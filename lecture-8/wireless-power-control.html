Wireless Power Control Game

N = 3 agents transmit on a shared channel. Agent i picks a transmit power γⁱ ∈ [pmin, pmax]. Raising your own power is costly but improves your own signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio; other agents' power only shows up in your cost as interference, which never depends on your own action γⁱ. That structural fact — your cost splits into "a piece that depends only on you" plus "a piece that depends only on everyone else" — is exactly the decoupled + dummy recipe from lecture, and it's why this game has an exact potential function.

Cost function
Jⁱ(γ) = C(γⁱ) − β·lnγⁱ + β·ln( n + α·Σj≠i γj )
Potential function
φ(γ) = Σⁱ [ C(γⁱ) − β·lnγⁱ ]
C(γⁱ) = ·γⁱ β = n = α = γⁱ ∈ [, ]

Each panel sweeps that player's own power γⁱ across [pmin, pmax] while holding the other two players' actions fixed at their current values, and plots both Jⁱ and φ along that sweep. Since the interference term β·ln(n+αΣj≠iγj) doesn't depend on γⁱ at all, moving the other players only slides the Jⁱ curve up or down — it never moves where the curve bottoms out. φ's curve bottoms out at the exact same γⁱ for the same reason. That shared minimizer, reached independently by every player, is the game's unique pure-strategy Nash equilibrium.
